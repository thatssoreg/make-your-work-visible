<h2>What can someone understand<br>without you explaining first?</h2>
<div class="exercise-layout"><div><p class="lead">Show your partner what exists now. Close the workshop’s template and use your own words when it is your turn to explain.</p><div class="peer-steps"><article><span>1</span><div><h3>The reader goes first.</h3><p>Read silently, then say: “I understand ___. I’m still looking for ___.” Point to something specific.</p></div></article><article><span>2</span><div><h3>The author explains a decision.</h3><p>Explain what you chose and why, without reading a sentence stem. Then switch roles.</p></div></article><article><span>3</span><div><h3>Both people revise one thing.</h3><p>Clarify a sentence, label an assumption, identify a missing piece, or improve the next step.</p></div></article></div><p class="small">For a solo version, close the template, explain one choice aloud or in writing, then read your work as someone new to it.</p><p class="continuation"><b>Keep:</b> One useful revision and one decision you can explain independently.</p></div><aside><div data-timer="peer"></div><div id="peer-phase" class="phase-note">Each partner has one minute; both use the last minute to revise.</div></aside></div>
