Projects help you learn.
A portfolio helps you explain that learning.

GitHub can hold and present your projects. Your portfolio is the work you choose to share and the context that helps someone understand it.

01

Fluency

You understand a field’s language, decisions, and problems more deeply because you have encountered the work.

02

Legibility

Other people can recognize your capability through the choices, contributions, evidence, and explanations you provide.

03

Reach

You give a relevant person enough context to engage with a particular question, finding, or piece of work.

The work builds fluency. The explanation builds legibility.

What are you starting with?

Choose the closest description, then write one possibility on each of three Post-it notes. Circle one to keep beside you for the rest of the session.

Note 1 · Revisit an assignment

Perhaps you have an assignment that could use a clearer chart, another code review, or a better explanation in its README.

Note 2 · Follow a question

Have you noticed that you run slower in the heat? Are you curious about egg prices, or why one music app seems to recommend better songs?

Note 3 · Try unfamiliar work

Do you wish you had more experience joining tables, working with APIs, or explaining a technical result to someone outside the field?

Keep for later: One circled note, or one question in your own document. You will return to it in each section.

Try it with your own work

Choose a plausible person or situation. In your own document, write two opening sentences that connect your work to a reason for talking.

Keep for later: Two sentences or a sharing plan. You can introduce a question you are exploring before you have a finished project.

Let’s work on your project.

Continue in your repository, notebook, document, or chosen tool. Improve the explanation, investigate the next question, or prepare a useful part of the work to share.

Bring to the reader test: Whatever exists now, such as a paragraph, plan, chart, change, or question. Keep planned work distinct from completed work.

Account trouble? Continue in a local document instead of spending the work period signing up.

What can someone understand
without you explaining first?

Show your partner what exists now. Close the workshop’s template and use your own words when it is your turn to explain.

1

The reader goes first.

Read silently, then say: “I understand ___. I’m still looking for ___.” Point to something specific.

2

The author explains a decision.

Explain what you chose and why, without reading a sentence stem. Then switch roles.

3

Both people revise one thing.

Clarify a sentence, label an assumption, identify a missing piece, or improve the next step.

For a solo version, close the template, explain one choice aloud or in writing, then read your work as someone new to it.

Keep: One useful revision and one decision you can explain independently.

Each partner has one minute; both use the last minute to revise.

What changed in your work today?

Save your file, note, or revision. Then name the next action you know how to take.
